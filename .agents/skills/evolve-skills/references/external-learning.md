# External Learning

Use this branch for substantive evolution reviews and explicit external-learning requests. Discover opportunities from the target's purpose as well as investigating known gaps. A new idea can justify a hypothesis without an observed failure; its usefulness still needs the evidence selected for that hypothesis.

## Explore from the purpose

Frame open questions about the outcomes and decisions the skill serves. Look for relevant methods in adjacent work before assuming the vocabulary of the answer. A request about reliable evaluation, for example, need not name a particular framework. Keep named evolution targets fixed; research expands possible methods, not the set of skills being edited.

Use relevant recent research first, checking whether its conditions remain applicable. Follow promising original material across tool designs, implementation accounts, talks, podcasts, technical blogs, research papers, and original articles or threads on social platforms such as X. Search for mechanisms and trade-offs, including alternatives to adding instructions. Recency can reveal new possibilities; it does not establish quality or applicability. Let relevance guide source choice rather than requiring a provider list or a quota of links.

Stop when the material supports a useful discussion of candidate methods, applicability and meaningful alternatives, and further searches repeat existing ideas. Continue a concrete lead when it could change the proposal. A bounded search may yield no supported opportunity; record its scope without claiming exhaustive frontier coverage. Respect unavailable access or user limits and continue independent work.

## Inspect and transfer

Use summaries and reposts to discover leads, then inspect the supporting original material before attributing a substantive claim. Follow a source's linked explanation or implementation when it could change the proposal. Preserve access limits: a synopsis, partial transcript or excerpt supports only what it contains. For cases, distinguish reported observations, demonstrated causes, fixes and inferred discovery methods; a closed issue or merged patch alone proves neither causality nor a fresh reproduction.

A failed fetch limits that retrieval method, not necessarily access to the source. When the source matters, try an available, authorized reading route suited to it, such as a browser for a dynamic article, before concluding it is unavailable. Honor the user's tool preferences and access boundaries. If suitable routes remain unavailable, retain the original link, what was actually read, and the unresolved limit; continue work supported by other evidence.

For each promising idea, identify the problem it addresses, its mechanism, operating assumptions and costs. Compare those with the target's actual method and history. Explain the decision that would change and the outcome it might improve. Preserve case-specific details as evidence rather than shared defaults. A speaker's authority, a memorable concept or similarity of vocabulary is insufficient evidence of transfer.

Turn the idea into a bounded hypothesis with an alternative explanation and a way to judge its intended value. Existing adequate guidance may mean the useful action is better execution, a focused experiment, removal of redundant instructions, or no change. Bring candidate methods into the consuming discussion and selection process before implementation.

## Make borrowing inspectable

Beside each externally informed proposal, give the original title or speaker/team and direct link, the specific borrowed idea, your adaptation, and the relevant applicability or evidence limits. Add a verified section or timestamp when it helps locate the passage; omit unavailable precision. Keep quotations distinct from paraphrases and inference. This is an explanation the user can correct, not a list of impressive references.

Retain source provenance with the evolution record under its established storage policy and link public reusable guidance only where it helps future use. Rejected leads need a short record when they explain a material decision; the user-facing proposal need not reproduce every search result. Source discovery supports hypothesis formation, while [purpose-led validation](validation.md) governs claims of usefulness.
